Small tool · runs entirely in this tab

Checkerboard → alpha

Paste an image whose “transparency” is actually a painted chequerboard (Ctrl+V) and get the same image back as a PNG with a real transparent background. Nothing is uploaded — the picture never leaves this window.

Paste an image to begin Ctrl+V (⌘V on a Mac) — or drop a file here, or choose one. You can paste again at any time.
What it actually does

It reads the two colours the chequerboard is made of and the size of its squares, then works out — pixel by pixel — how much ink sits on the square underneath, and writes that as the pixel's opacity. Anything at or lighter than the darker square colour is treated as empty, which is what removes the seams between squares in images that arrived as a JPEG. If the picture already has a real transparent background it is passed through untouched, and if it turns out to be a flat colour instead of a chequerboard, the flat colour is keyed out.

Copy PNG (or Ctrl+C) puts the result on the clipboard, ready to paste into Keynote, Figma, Illustrator… Download or Ctrl+S saves the file, and Trim faint pixels drops the soft halo a JPEG leaves around edges — raise it a little if you can still see a ghost of the squares.

A page opened straight off your disk is a special case for browsers, and some of them will not let it touch the clipboard. When that happens the tool says so and saves the PNG to your Downloads folder instead — same file, one step more. The line under the dimensions always tells you which one just happened.
